## When a client asks about exceeding the Kleinunternehmer threshold

**Step 1 — Establish which threshold is in question.**
Ask the client to clarify: is this about last year's turnover (the €25,000 prior-year test) or this year's running total (the €100,000 current-year test)? Clients frequently conflate the two.

**Step 2 — Request the following from the client before drafting any response:**
- Total turnover received so far in the current calendar year (Ist-Umsatz, not invoiced-but-unpaid amounts).
- Total turnover for the prior calendar year, if not already on file.
- Date and amount of the transaction that triggered the client's concern, if applicable.

**Step 3 — Determine which scenario applies.**
- If the client's *current-year* turnover has crossed €100,000: status ends immediately, from the triggering transaction onward. The client must begin charging VAT on invoices from that point and register for regular VAT filing (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung).
- If the client's *prior-year* turnover exceeded €25,000: status ends from 1 January of the following year, regardless of the current year's figures.
- If neither threshold is crossed: reassure the client that Kleinunternehmer status continues, but recommend monitoring turnover monthly rather than waiting for the annual review.

**Step 4 — Standard next actions for our firm:**
- If status has ended: schedule a call to walk through VAT registration and invoicing changes — do not handle this by email alone.
- Update the client's file in DATEV to reflect the change in tax status.
- Send the client our standard "Kleinunternehmer status change" information sheet (internal ref: KU-INFO-01, not included in this demo).

**Step 5 — What NOT to say in a first response.**
Do not confirm a client's VAT registration date or exact filing obligations without a partner's review — this checklist supports drafting a first response, not a final one. Any drafted reply must be marked for staff review before sending.

## Common client phrasing to recognize

Clients rarely use the precise legal terms. Typical ways this question arrives:
- "Do I need to charge tax now?"
- "I think I made too much money this year — what does that mean for me?"
- "Someone told me I have to register for VAT now."

All of these map to the threshold-check workflow above.
